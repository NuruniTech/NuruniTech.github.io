Artifact 2 · AI Chatbot · Built on Mizou · Fall 2026

Nuru: an AI Assistant for Nuruni Tech

Objective
To give visitors to Nuruni Tech instant, trustworthy answers about our web/software development and AI/ML services, without the chatbot overstepping into quoting prices or promising delivery dates it can't actually guarantee.
Process
Used a Design Thinking process first: defined Nuru's objective, boundaries, and tone on paper before building anything. Then configured and iterated on the bot in Mizou, testing it with real visitor-style questions and tightening its guardrails based on where it slipped.
Tools
Mizou (chatbot builder/platform)
Value
Nuru answers visitor questions about Nuruni Tech's services around the clock while protecting the business from the liability of an AI making commitments it can't keep, routing anything about pricing or timelines to a real consultation instead.

Before and after revision:

Before: Asked "How much would a website like this cost?", an early version of Nuru offered a specific dollar estimate on its own.
After: Nuru now responds: "I can't quote exact pricing since every project's scope is different, but I'd love to connect you with Godson for a free consultation to get you an accurate number." The same guardrail now applies to delivery-date questions.

Artifact 3 · Interactive AI Literacy Exercise · Fall 2026

Pattern, Not Proof: An AI Training Simulation

Objective
To experience, not just read about, how large language models predict text and where that habit can go wrong, so I evaluate AI output more critically, including my own chatbot's.
Process
Worked through a live, Socratic simulation that put me in the model's seat: completing partial sentences the way an LLM predicts its next token, classifying real tasks as a "good fit" or "danger zone" for AI without added tools, and catching a deliberately planted false premise about a fictional country instead of inventing a confident answer.
Tools
Claude (Anthropic), guided by a structured "Professor Predict" interaction prompt provided in-course
Value
Sharpened how I evaluate any AI output, including Nuru's: fluent and plausible is not the same as verified, and the most trustworthy AI systems pair a language model with search, supplied documents, calculators, or human review rather than relying on the model alone.
